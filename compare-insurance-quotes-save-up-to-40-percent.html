Why Compare Insurance Quotes Online?

According to JD Power's 2026 U.S. Insurance Digital Experience Study, nearly half (47%) of all new auto and home insurance policies are now purchased digitally . Yet many consumers still don't shop around—and it's costing them billions.

Research from Go.Compare reveals that 45% of consumers don't bother shopping around for insurance when their renewal is due. The result? An estimated £8.7 billion in potential savings is left on the table . Simply put, the most expensive insurance policy is the one you renew without comparing alternatives.

The Auto-Renewal Trap: Insurance companies count on customer inertia. Many providers quietly increase premiums at renewal, hoping you won't notice. Comparison shopping is the only way to ensure you're not overpaying by hundreds of dollars each year .

How Comparison Sites Make Money (It's Free for You)

All the platforms listed above are completely free for consumers. You never pay to use their comparison tools .

These companies make money through commissions. When you purchase an insurance policy through the platform, the insurance carrier pays a commission. This comes from the carrier's marketing budget—not an upcharge to you. Your rates are the same as if you went directly to the carrier .

How to Compare Quotes Effectively

Step 1: Gather Your Information

Before starting, have ready:

  • Driver's license number
  • Vehicle VIN for each car
  • Current insurance policy details (for accurate comparison)
  • Annual mileage estimates
  • Home address and property details (for home insurance)

Step 2: Compare Apples to Apples

When comparing quotes, ensure you're comparing identical coverage limits, deductibles, and policy periods. A cheaper quote with lower coverage limits isn't actually a better deal .

Step 3: Check Multiple Platforms

Different comparison sites partner with different carriers. Use 2-3 platforms to ensure you're seeing the full market .

Step 4: Review the Insurer, Not Just the Price

Check:

  • Financial strength ratings (AM Best)
  • Customer satisfaction scores (JD Power)
  • Claim settlement ratios
  • Available discounts (bundling, safe driver, good student)

Watch Out for Lead Generators: Some comparison sites operate as lead generators, selling your information to multiple agents who will call you repeatedly. Look for platforms that provide "real-time" or "bindable" quotes—these typically don't resell your data .


What Information Do Comparison Sites Access?

Legitimate comparison platforms need certain information to provide accurate quotes:

  • Driving record and claims history — To assess risk accurately
  • Credit-based insurance score — In states where permitted (varies by state)
  • Vehicle information — Make, model, year, safety features
  • Home characteristics — Age, construction, security features, claims history

JD Power research found that just one-third of insurance shoppers encounter comparison pricing tools that include other insurance brands, and customers are nearly two times as likely to consider purchasing a policy when price comparisons are provided (39%) than when no price comparison tools are available (21%) .

When to Compare Insurance Quotes

Industry experts recommend comparing quotes:

  • At every renewal — Usually every 6-12 months
  • After major life changes — Moving, getting married, adding a teen driver, buying a home
  • When your credit score improves — Can significantly lower premiums
  • After a ticket falls off your record — Usually after 3-5 years
  • When you pay off your car loan — You may choose to drop collision/comprehensive
