Does Full Coverage Insurance Cover Hit-and-Run? What You Need to Know in 2025

MC
Michael Chen
Auto Insurance Specialist
October 8, 2025
9 min read

Coming back to your car to find it damaged with no note or responsible party in sight is frustrating and stressful. Hit-and-run accidents are increasingly common, with approximately 17% of California drivers being uninsured according to recent data . The immediate question most drivers ask is: "Will my full coverage insurance protect me?"

The answer is generally yes, but with important caveats about deductibles, coverage types, and state-specific regulations. Understanding exactly how your policy applies to hit-and-run situations can save you thousands of dollars and significant frustration.

What "Full Coverage" Really Means for Hit-and-Runs

While "full coverage" isn't a technical insurance term, it typically refers to a policy that includes both comprehensive and collision coverage in addition to state-required liability insurance. Here's how these coverages apply to hit-and-run situations:

Primary Coverage

Collision Coverage

This is your primary protection for hit-and-run vehicle damage. Collision coverage pays for repairs to your vehicle regardless of who is at fault, making it essential when the responsible driver cannot be identified .

Key point: You'll need to pay your deductible before coverage applies, typically ranging from $250 to $1,000.

Injury Protection

Uninsured Motorist Coverage

When the hit-and-run driver cannot be identified, they're effectively "uninsured" from your perspective. Uninsured Motorist (UM) coverage can help pay for injuries to you and your passengers .

California note: Insurers must offer UM coverage, but you can decline it in writing—though this isn't recommended.

Additional Protection

Medical Payments Coverage

Also known as MedPay, this optional coverage helps pay for medical expenses for you and your passengers regardless of who caused the accident . It can be particularly valuable when you have health insurance with high deductibles.

Hit-and-Run Coverage Scenarios

How your insurance responds depends heavily on the specific circumstances of the hit-and-run and the coverages you carry.

Immediate Steps After a Hit-and-Run

Taking the right actions immediately after discovering hit-and-run damage significantly improves your chances of a successful insurance claim and potentially identifying the responsible party.

Critical Steps to Protect Yourself and Your Claim

1

Document the Scene Thoroughly

Take comprehensive photos of the damage, your vehicle's position, and the surrounding area. Look for security cameras that might have captured the incident .

2

File a Police Report Immediately

Contact local law enforcement to file an official hit-and-run report. Many insurers require a police report for hit-and-run claims, and this creates an official record of the incident .

3

Notify Your Insurance Company Promptly

Contact your insurer as soon as possible—many have specific time limits for reporting hit-and-run incidents. Provide them with the police report number and all documentation you've gathered .

4

Seek Medical Attention if Injured

Even if injuries seem minor, get medical evaluation immediately. Some symptoms appear hours or days later, and medical documentation is crucial for injury claims .

"Many hit-and-run collisions may seem minor, like a bumper tap, and at-fault drivers often believe you won't bother taking action. However, minor collisions can still cause serious issues that might not surface until months later." — Insurance Claims Expert

Understanding Deductibles and Out-of-Pocket Costs

How Deductibles Work in Hit-and-Run Claims

When you use collision coverage for hit-and-run damage, you're responsible for paying your deductible before insurance covers the remaining repair costs .

$500 Average Collision Deductible
$250 Common UM Property Damage Deductible
$0-$250 UM Bodily Injury Deductible

Example: If repairs cost $2,500 and you have a $500 deductible, you pay $500 and your insurance covers $2,000. If the hit-and-run driver is eventually identified, you may be able to recover your deductible from their insurance company.

Common Myths About Hit-and-Run Coverage

Myth: "I'm completely covered since it wasn't my fault"

Reality: While full coverage typically applies, you're still responsible for your deductible. Additionally, some policies have specific limitations for unidentified driver claims .

Myth: "Filing a claim will definitely raise my rates"

Reality: Many states prohibit rate increases for not-at-fault claims, and some insurers won't raise rates for first-time hit-and-run claims where you weren't at fault .

Myth: "I don't need a police report for minor damage"

Reality: Most insurers require a police report for hit-and-run claims regardless of damage severity. The report creates crucial documentation for your claim .

Will Your Premiums Increase After a Hit-and-Run Claim?

This is one of the most common concerns for hit-and-run victims. The answer depends on your insurer's policies and your state's regulations:

  • Not-at-fault protection: Many states have laws that prevent insurers from raising rates for drivers who are not at fault in accidents
  • Insurer variations: Some companies may still increase premiums after any claim, while others distinguish between at-fault and not-at-fault incidents
  • Claim frequency: Multiple claims within a short period may affect your rates regardless of fault
  • Discount impact: You might lose accident-free discounts, effectively increasing your premium

The best approach is to discuss potential rate impacts with your agent before filing a claim, so you can make an informed decision.

Key Takeaways

  • Full coverage insurance typically covers hit-and-run damage through collision coverage, but you must pay your deductible
  • Uninsured Motorist coverage is crucial for hit-and-run injuries and may offer better protection than health insurance
  • Always file a police report immediately—most insurers require it for hit-and-run claims
  • Document everything thoroughly with photos and witness statements to support your claim
  • Premium increases after hit-and-run claims vary by insurer and state regulations
  • If the hit-and-run driver is later identified, you may recover your deductible from their insurance

Hit-and-run accidents are frustrating, but having the right insurance coverage and knowing how to navigate the claims process can significantly reduce the financial and emotional impact. By understanding exactly how your full coverage policy applies to these situations and taking prompt, appropriate action, you can ensure you're protected when the unexpected occurs.
